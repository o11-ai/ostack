# Writing first touches

The playbook's `voice.md` decides how messages sound, `offer.md` decides what is offered and what the ask is, and `writing` sets hard limits enforced by `reserve`. The sequence step's `brief` says what this particular touch should do. This file covers the method.

## Shape

1. **Opening:** one or two sentences built on the observed fact from research, connected to a specific task or decision the recipient has. The reader should immediately see why you wrote to *them*.
2. **Offer:** one sentence from `offer.md`, in the recipient's terms. It describes the outcome, not your product's internals.
3. **Ask:** exactly one, as stated in `offer.md` (for example a short call or a yes/no question). Never a signup or trial unless the offer says so.
4. **Signature:** `sender.signature` from the playbook, exactly.

The subject (email) is short, lowercase-friendly and specific to them. It is a plain question or phrase a colleague might send, never a campaign headline. Never fake `re:`/`fwd:` or pretend to be a customer or a support issue.

LinkedIn messages and connection notes are shorter: one or two lines, no signature block, and no subject.

## Quality bar

Read the subject and opening together and check:

1. Is every fact from a source you actually read in research?
2. Swap test: if you replace the company name with a competitor's, does the message still work? If yes, it is too generic. Do the research again or defer the lead.
3. Does the opening point at a concrete task, account type or decision, not a feature recap or generic praise?
4. Is unknown pain asked, not asserted? ("what's it costing you to..." rather than "you're overpaying for...")
5. Does it follow every rule in `writing.rules` and the tone in `voice.md`?
6. Is every placeholder filled?

If the angle is weak, say so (for previews) or defer the lead (for runs). Do not invent a stronger one.

## Variety

Across a batch, vary openings and hooks naturally. Never rotate one template with noun swaps; each message should read as written for that person.

## Previews

When the user asks for examples, show for each: the company and URL, two sentences on what they do, who the message is for and why they qualify (or which gaps remain), then the full subject and body. Mark any lead whose qualification is incomplete.
